Make a single self-contained HTML file: a small third-person 3D game scene called "hollin water", where the world starts as a pencil underdrawing on white paper and watercolour spreads out from wherever you walk. Use three.js 0.170 from a CDN through an import map. No other libraries, no downloaded models, textures, fonts or sounds: everything is made in code.

THE PLACE
Hollin Water, an invented lake in the hills, late on a summer afternoon. A grassy slope runs down to the near shore, edged with a strip of pale sand and clumps of reeds. A wooden jetty walks thirty metres out over the water on posts, with a lantern at its end, water lilies either side and a teal rowing boat tied up beside it, rocking. To the east, an orchard of apple trees in loose rows (red and yellow apples, a few still in pink blossom), a post-and-rail fence on its lake side and three white beehives in its far corner. To the west, a small cream cottage with a slate-blue roof, a red door, shuttered windows with flower boxes, a water butt, a bench and a picket fence round its front garden, a big oak beside it and birches along the shore. A sandy path wanders from the jetty's foot up through the meadow and away south, with a branch to the cottage door. Clumps of wild flowers everywhere: poppies, cornflowers, buttercups, daisies. Across the lake, wooded hills; behind you, rolling fields and copses into the haze.

THE LOOK
This is the heart of the piece; if something has to give, keep this.
- Do it in the surface shaders: light everything with ordinary Lambert lighting and shadows, then, at the end of each material's fragment shader, take how lit the pixel is relative to its own flat colour and repaint it.
- Unpainted, the world is a graphite underdrawing on white paper: nearly colourless, shade drawn as hatching that lives on the surfaces (the level lines of a height measured along one fixed direction in the world, spacing held near 4 pixels by blending octaves), crossed with a second direction where it is darkest, broken up by a world-space paper tooth. Grass is drawn as faint short strokes, the lake as loose horizontal ripple lines.
- The wash map: a float texture over the ground seen from above, 256 cells across 128 metres. Every cell stores how covered it is and the time colour first reached it. As the walker moves, stamp cells around them out to three or four metres (a radius wobbled by noise, with a soft margin), each with "now plus distance times 0.4 s", so the colour runs outward from the footsteps.
- Every vertex carries a root: where its colour comes from. The ground and walls use the ground straight below them, trees and flowers a point at their foot, the far fields the nearest place you could have walked, the far hills and open water the end of the jetty. The age of the paint is now minus the time at the root, minus a climb (colour creeps up walls and into tree crowns a moment later), minus a few octaves of noise so the advancing edge runs ahead here and hangs back there. It never goes away.
- Blend from sketch to paint with that age. The paint is watercolour: in the light, paper left nearly bare under a warm glaze; pigment density varying in big soft blooms; a darker wet rim at the advancing edge that dries over a few seconds, and a faint dried tide-line wherever a wash stopped; a cool violet glaze in shade; far things paler and cooler; the paper's tooth in everything. Painted grass is gone over in short strokes lighter-yellower and darker-cooler, lying away from the eye. Painted water gets drifting streaks and paper-white glints.
- The sky is bare paper with a few pencil lines for clouds until you reach the end of the jetty or have painted a fair patch; then it is washed in from the lake upward: gold low toward the sun, rose, then clear blue, clouds left as paper with lavender under them.
- Then one pass over the image: soft wobbly graphite outlines from depth and flat-colour changes (always at the skyline), paper grain, and a soft watery white margin with a faint tide line just inside it. A small handwritten caption in a corner names where you are ("hollin water, the jetty").
- The last wash: after two minutes of walking, or once you have painted most of the land, a final wave spreads out from where you stand across everything to the horizon.

THE WALKER
A small painter built from primitives: mustard smock with a pocket, red scarf, teal beret, navy trousers, brown boots, a little blue paintbox on a strap and a long brush across the back with a red tip. They are always in colour. Arms and legs swing as they walk, faster when they run; they tuck their legs when they jump. They turn to face the way they move.

Before the first click, a slow camera drift along the shore and a "click to walk" hint; the drift paints a little around where the camera looks, so the paper is never quite blank. On click: third-person camera behind the walker, mouse to look (pointer lock, drag as a fallback), WASD to move relative to the camera, Shift to run, Space to jump, mouse wheel to zoom. They collide with the cottage, trees, hives, fence and bench, can't walk into the lake but can walk out along the jetty. The camera pulls in when something gets between it and them. Hide the hint when the page is loaded with ?thumb=1.

Keep it smooth on a laptop: merge the static world into one mesh, upload the wash map only when it changes, cap the pixel ratio and lower it if frames get slow. Handle window resize.
