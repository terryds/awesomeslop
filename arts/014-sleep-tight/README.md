# sleep tight

A 60-second TV commercial for NYENYAK, a mosquito coil that never existed, as it might have aired on Indonesian television in the mid-'90s and survived on somebody's tape. A gang of mosquitoes in sunglasses, a father who can't sleep, and smoke that makes a fist. It loops, bumpers and all.

## what you're looking at

Nothing here is an image, a video or a sound file. Each frame is drawn three times over.

- **First, a small cartoon.** The ad itself is drawn at 640 × 480: flat colours, dark marker outlines that are re-wobbled 8 times a second, fat block letters with a hard drop, comic bursts and spinning sunbursts. Night is one multiply tint over the daytime bedroom.
- **Then the tape.** The frame is copied in strips four pixels tall, each nudged sideways by a hair. One band of tracking noise crawls up the picture, the bottom rows are torn the way a VCR tears them, and every cut gets a short burst of tearing. The blacks are lifted towards purple and grain goes on top.
- **Then the tube.** The picture is split into a red copy and a cyan copy and added back together a pixel or so apart, which gives the colour fringes on every edge. A faint ghost sits to the right. Scanlines, a vignette, a glare and the rounded, slightly bulging screen are drawn once and laid over each frame.
- **One timeline, ten shots.** Each shot is a function of its own local time. The slaps, the punches and the syllables of the sing-along sit at fixed beats, and the sound effects are scheduled from the same numbers.
- **The jingle is oscillators and noise.** A kendang-style "dung" and "tak", bass, organ on the off-beats and a square lead. The sing-along reuses the tune you already heard under the smoke scene, one note per syllable, with an ember as the bouncing ball. The picture runs off the audio clock, so they stay together after a pause or a skip.

## things worth stealing from the prompt

- **Draw small, then damage it in two separate passes.** Tape faults (wobble, tearing, tracking) and tube faults (colour fringes, scanlines, curvature) are different things. Doing both to a low-resolution picture is most of the look.
- **Say what the ad is selling and write the lines.** Naming the product, the slogan and the fine print gets you a real commercial structure: problem, villain, product, demonstration, jingle, pack shot.
- **Put the place in the props.** The jalousie window, the shop calendar, the sarong, the flip-flops and the covered glass of tea do more than any caption to say where this is.
- **Give the jingle the syllables.** One note per syllable, on the beat, makes the sing-along panel and the tune line up without any tuning.

## limits

- The sound needs a click (browsers insist). Skipping restarts the music from the new spot, so a long note that was already ringing is dropped.
- There is no voice-over. The jingle is sung by a synth and the words are on screen.
- The lettering uses whatever heavy system font is there (Arial Black where it exists), so it looks a little different from one machine to the next.
- The fine print is hard to read on purpose, and harder on a small screen.
