Make a single self-contained HTML file: a small third-person action RPG scene called "sword of the yellow wood": an autumn wood that looks like an oil painting, which you walk through with a sword, with bears to fight and things to find. Keep the fighting light: the place is still the point. Use three.js 0.170 from a CDN through an import map. No other libraries, no downloaded models, textures, fonts or sound files: everything is made in code, the sound included (WebAudio).

THE LOOK
This is the heart of the piece; if something has to give, keep this. Do not render a normal scene and filter it. Paint the world: every surface is covered in brush strokes that are fixed in the world, and the frame goes to the screen with no post-processing at all.
- A stroke is one flat instanced quad in one flat colour, cut to a shape in its fragment shader. Three shapes: a dab of a flat brush (seven bristle streaks each a little lighter or darker, ragged ends where each bristle stops at its own length, a light ridge of thick paint down one long edge and a dark one down the other), a leaf (one pointed touch, lighter on one half, with a dark rib), and a blade (drawn from the root up, tapering).
- Two kinds of stroke. Fixed strokes lie on a surface with their own direction: ground, bark, stone, cloth, sky. Facing strokes turn toward the viewer round a point that never moves: leaves and blades.
- Three paints per thing: a shadow colour, a middle and a light. Each stroke takes one of the three by how lit its spot is, plus a little chance, then shifts it slightly in hue and lightness. Never blend between them.
- Stroke order must never depend on the camera, or the paint crawls when you move. So: no transparency and no sorting. Every stroke writes depth, edges are smoothed with alpha-to-coverage, and each stroke is lifted off its surface by its own fixed random amount (growing a little with distance), which decides once and for all which stroke is on top.
- Under the strokes, plain shapes in the darkest paint (the ground, trunks, boulders, the walker's body), so no gap ever shows through.
- Shadows are painted, not computed per frame. Once, at the start, draw every tree's shadow on a hidden 2D canvas seen from above: a long line for the trunk and ovals for the clumps of leaves, pushed along the ground away from the sun by their height. Every ground stroke reads that picture at its centre and takes either the sunlit paints or the shadow paints.
- Haze: each stroke fades toward a pale lilac with distance, turning warm gold when you look toward the sun. Work it out once per stroke, so a stroke stays one flat colour.
- Colour: gold, orange, russet and crimson, with plum and violet in the shadows and a pale blue sky. Light paints: #ffe070, #f2b42a, #d8851a. Shadow paints: #4a2a50, #7c3a46, #a9522e.

THE WOOD
A round wood 240 m across on gently rolling ground, in the last hour of sun (the sun about 24 degrees up, so shadows are more than twice as long as the things that throw them).
- Ground: about 130,000 dabs, each roughly a metre long, laid along the direction the shadows fall, wandering with noise. Over them 40,000 fallen leaves as single bright touches.
- About 720 trees, 11 to 19 m tall, leaning slightly, none closer than 3.3 m: three in five are birches (cream and warm white in the sun, lilac-grey in shade, short dark marks across the bark), the rest dark-barked (plum, with orange-gold where the sun hits). Bark is long upright strokes, lit on the sun side and dappled lower down. Four to six thin branches reach out and up; a clump of about 22 leaves sits at the end of each and three more at the top. Each tree has one leaf colour: mostly gold or orange, some russet, a few still green, a few crimson. Leaves stir in the wind.
- In a clearing in the middle, one old tree twice as thick as the rest, all crimson, with a carpet of red leaves under it.
- A footpath of paler, pinker earth loops through the wood.
- Thin saplings with leaves at eye level, about 3,600 tufts of fern and grass (yellow, orange, a few dark green), and sixteen lilac-grey boulders with moss on top.
- Leaves fall all the time, tumbling, in a box of air that follows you.
- Sky: a dome of 2,800 very large strokes swept in rings round the sun: pale blue above, cream cloud, a gold glow and a white sun low down. It travels with you. The edge of the wood is lost in haze, and you are turned back 60 m from the middle so you never reach it.

THE WALKER
A small figure made of the same strokes over simple shapes: a long viridian coat, a cream scarf with a loose end, auburn hair in a bun, dark trousers, brown boots, and a sword a metre long in one hand, on its own joint at the wrist (a tapered steel stroke, twice, crossed, with a gold guard), held pointing forward and a little down. Because it moves, its strokes pick their paint every frame from the sun's direction, and go to the shadow paint when it walks into a tree's shadow. Arms and legs swing as it walks, more when it runs; the scarf's loose end hangs down its back and lifts behind as it speeds up. It throws its own long shadow, a few dark strokes on the ground, which disappears inside other shadows.

Before the first click it follows the footpath by itself while the camera drifts behind it, low to the ground, with a "click to walk" hint. On click: third-person camera, mouse to look (pointer lock, drag as a fallback; without pointer lock a tap that is not a drag swings the sword), W A S D to walk relative to the camera, Shift to run, Space to jump. It turns to face the way it moves and cannot walk through trunks or boulders.

SWORD, BEARS, FINDS
The fighting has to be felt. Every blow that lands should stop, shake and sound.
- The combo. Left click (or F) swings. Three swings chain: a level slash one way, a backhand slash back the other way, then a leaping overhead chop. A click during a swing queues the next one, which starts 60% of the way through the current swing; a click within half a second of a swing ending also continues the chain. Slashes take 0.36 s and the chop 0.58 s, each with a short wind-up, a fast cut and a recovery. The sword arm sweeps round the shoulder with the body turning into it; for the chop the arm goes right back over the head, the walker hops, and the body leans in.
- Each swing first turns the walker to the nearest bear within 5 m, or to where the camera looks if there is none, and steps them forward. While swinging they barely move otherwise.
- A slash hits every bear in front within 1.7 m of its body and does 1; the chop reaches 2.1 m and does 2.
- When a blow lands: the game freezes for 65 ms (140 ms for the chop, 200 ms for a kill), the camera shakes, the bear flashes pale, squashes and is knocked back (twice as far by the chop), and ten or twenty dabs of paint fly off it and fall to the ground. The chop also kicks up leaves where it lands, hit or miss.
- The blade leaves paint in the air: while it cuts, lay a pale cream stroke each frame between where the blade was and where it is now (gold for the chop), and let each one thin away over 0.2 s. These and the flying dabs are two small pools of strokes updated by hand.
- Dodge: right click or E dashes 3 m the way you are moving, or hops straight back if you are standing still. Nothing can hurt you during it. It cancels a swing. 0.7 s between dodges.
- Eight bears made of strokes like everything else: a long round body, a head with a tan muzzle, dark nose and eyes, round ears, four legs that swing as they walk. Seven brown ones are spread through the wood and have 5 health. One old, near-black bear, half as big again, keeps the clearing under the red tree and has 14. Each bear has its own material, so each goes dark in shadow by itself. Bears are solid: you cannot walk through one.
- A bear wanders slowly round its own patch, stopping now and then. When you come within 13 m (or hit it) it runs at you, a little faster than you walk and slower than you run. In reach it growls and rears up for half a second, front paws high: that is the warning. Then it lunges and swipes; if you are still in front of it you lose a heart and are thrown back, with a red flash at the edge of the screen, a freeze and a hard shake. Two seconds between swipes. It gives up beyond 28 m.
- Hitting a brown bear staggers it for 0.4 s (0.75 s for the chop) and knocks it out of a swipe it has started. The old bear is only staggered by the chop, and never once it has reared up, so its swipe must be dodged.
- A small red health bar floats over the bear you last hit. A dead bear rolls over and sinks away, and another comes back to its patch 45 seconds later if you are not near.
- You have five hearts. At none you fall over among the leaves, and wake on the footpath with all five.
- Fourteen red mushrooms with cream spots grow in the wood: walking over one mends a heart (only if you are missing one), and it grows back in 50 seconds. Twenty golden acorns float and turn at knee height, most of them near the footpath and three under the red tree: walk through them to collect them.
- Sound, all synthesised: a rising whoosh of filtered noise for each swing (lower and longer for the chop), a low sine thump plus a crack of noise for a hit, a heavier one for the chop, a low sawtooth growl when a bear rears, a falling tone when it dies, a soft rustle for each footstep, two high notes for an acorn, a rising note for a mushroom. M turns it off.
- On screen, top left, in small serif type: the hearts, "acorns 3 / 20", and "bears felled 2". Short messages fade in near the top ("a golden acorn", "the old bear is down").
- The bears ignore the walker until the visitor has clicked.
