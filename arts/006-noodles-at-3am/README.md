# noodles at 3 a.m.

A neon street in the rain, painted in ten seconds from about 60,000 brush marks. No images.

**Click it** to paint another one. Each version has different buildings and signs, and it alternates between magenta-and-cyan and amber-and-teal.

## what you're looking at

Same method as [the long way home](/art/005-the-long-way-home), pointed at a much harder subject: a picture that is almost entirely darkness, where the only thing worth looking at is light.

- **The glow is in the hidden picture, not the brush.** Before any mark is made, each sign adds a soft halo of its colour to the flat reference drawing. The brush then just copies what it finds, so walls near a pink sign come out pink without any special case.
- **Reflections are geometry.** A sign reappears in the road below the spot where it would touch the ground, flipped and stretched. That is the difference between "wet street" and "coloured stripes".
- **The road is painted twice, in two directions.** Long vertical marks first, which smear the reflections downward. Then short horizontal ones on top, which break them into ripples.
- **One warm light.** Everything is magenta, cyan and violet except the noodle stand. That is why your eye finds it.

The lettering is invented: each glyph is three to five random bars. No real words or brands.

## things worth stealing from the prompt

- **"Everything is dark except the light."** One sentence that sets the value structure for the whole picture.
- **Say how a reflection works**, not just that there is one: "starting under the point where the sign would touch the ground and fading toward us."
- **Give the brush two jobs on one surface.** "Long vertical marks first, then short horizontal ones on top."
- **Ban the obvious failure.** "No real words, no real brands" stops the agent from writing half-remembered signage.
