# north light

An aurora over a fjord, drawn on dark paper with soft pastel, oil pastel, coloured pencil and chalk. Nothing is a picture: the page is about 21,000 strokes, laid down one after another while you watch.

**Click** while it is drawing to finish it at once. Click again for another one: same place, different hand.

## what you're looking at

- **Paper with a tooth.** One small height field stands for the grain of the sheet. Every batch of strokes is drawn on a spare canvas, has the valleys of that grain knocked out of it, and only then goes on the page. Because the grain never moves, the same specks of bare paper show through every layer, which is what makes it read as pastel and not as a brush in an app.
- **Strokes are ribbons.** Each one has a spine that wanders, a ragged edge and more weight in the middle than at the ends. Pastel is broad and misses a fifth of the tooth; oil pastel is narrow and nearly solid; pencil is a line.
- **A box of sticks, not a colour picker.** Each area has a short row of colours. Between two neighbours the drawing picks one or the other, never a blend, so the gradients are made the way a person makes them: by hatching two sticks into each other.
- **Hatching with a direction.** Sky on a diagonal, mountain faces down their slope, water and snow level, the roof along its pitch.
- **The aurora** is three winding paths with rays rising toward one point far above the page. Each ray is a handful of short strokes, white at the hem and magenta at the crown. The same rays are laid on the water afterwards as broken dashes.

## things worth stealing from the prompt

- **Name the tools and what each one can't do.** "Catches about four fifths of the tooth" does more than "make it look like pastel".
- **Fix the grain to the paper, not to the stroke.** One mask for the whole sheet.
- **Forbid blending.** "Pick one stick or the other" is the line that gets rid of the digital gradient.
- **Give every surface a hatching direction.**
- **Ask for the order.** Sky first, last touches last: the build-up is half the pleasure.

## limits

- It is a drawing, not a scene: nothing moves once it is finished.
- It is always 2400 x 1350, scaled to the window.
