# quarter turns

Truchet tiles: one tile, four rotations, endless plumbing.

## what you're looking at

Every square holds the same two arcs. Each arc starts and ends at the middle of a side, so whichever way a tile is rotated, its arcs always meet the arcs next door. No tile knows anything about its neighbours, yet the whole wall reads as continuous loops.

A tile turns every tenth of a second. One quarter turn reroutes two paths at once, so a single orange flicker can merge two loops into one or snap one loop in two.

## things worth stealing from the prompt

- **Describing the rule, not the picture.** The prompt never says "draw loops". It describes one tile and one rule, and explains *why* loops emerge. Agents do better when they understand the mechanism.
- **"Flat ends so arcs meet cleanly"** heads off the most common flaw: round caps that bulge at every tile border.
- **Colour only while moving.** The orange is how you find the change in a busy field.

## try changing

- turn two arcs into a diagonal line for the classic maze version
- flip tiles in a travelling wave instead of at random
- fill the regions between paths with two alternating colours
