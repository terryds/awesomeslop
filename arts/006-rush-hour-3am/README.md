# rush hour, 3 a.m.

A packed neon street in the rain, painted in eleven seconds from about 100,000 brush marks. No images.

**Click it** to paint another one. Each version has a different street and a different crowd, and it alternates between magenta-and-cyan and amber-and-teal.

## what you're looking at

Same method as [the long way home](/art/005-the-long-way-home): a plain flat picture is drawn off-screen, then the visible one is built from brush marks that copy its colours. The subject here is a crowd.

- **Everyone stands on the same ground.** About 115 people are placed at random depths, and each one's size comes from how far away they are. That one rule is why it reads as a crowd in a street instead of figures pasted on a backdrop.
- **We look from above head height.** From a footbridge, the crowd spreads down the picture and you see umbrella tops. At eye level the same crowd would collapse into one row of heads.
- **People are seen against light.** The shopfronts at street level and the wet ground are bright; the people are dark. Take the shopfronts away and the crowd disappears.
- **Stalls are part of the crowd.** Eight vendor stalls stand against the walls. They are sorted by distance together with the people and drawn in one pass, so walkers cross in front of a stall and the stall covers whoever is behind it.
- **No two buildings alike.** Width, height and tint are random for each one, and each gets one of four facades: window grid, office strips, a dark slab with neon edges, or a giant screen. Where a tall one stands behind a short one, you see its end wall.
- **The glow is in the hidden picture, not the brush.** Each sign adds a halo of its colour to the reference, so walls and umbrellas near a pink sign come out pink with no special case.

The lettering is invented: each glyph is three to five random bars. No real words or brands.

## the first version was wrong

This piece started as "noodles at 3 a.m.": an empty street, one walker, a noodle stand. It looked odd, because an empty neon street has nothing in it to look at. The fix was not better brushwork. It was the subject: fill the street.

## things worth stealing from the prompt

- **Say what the subject is.** "The crowd is the subject." Without it, an agent spends its effort on buildings.
- **Say where the viewer stands.** "On a footbridge a little above head height" decides the whole composition.
- **Give the one rule that makes a crowd.** "All standing on the same ground so they shrink together."
- **Say what the people are seen against.** Dark figures need a bright ground.
